How to use it

Be the firm they already know.

  1. Start six months out, and push hardest from three.That is when owners are meeting firms. HDB gives each owner their key appointment no more than two weeks ahead, and by then most have chosen.
  2. Show the layout they're getting.Every flat in a BTO project follows one of a few floor plans, printed in HDB's sales brochure for the project. A finished flat or a 3D of that exact plan says more than a general portfolio.
  3. Aim at the estate, not the island.Facebook and Instagram ads can be limited to people near a place. Name the project and the town in the ad, and show work you've done nearby.
  4. Keep to the rules on flyers and messages.Pasting flyers on HDB common property needs the Town Council's permission, and HDB warns residents about contractors selling door to door. A marketing message to a Singapore mobile needs a Do Not Call check, unless the owner has agreed to hear from you.

Where this comes from

Each project's dates come from HDB: the estimated completion it gave when the project launched, or a later update HDB has published. Where HDB's own launch tables leave the estimate out, it comes from launch-week reviews that quoted HDB's sales site. HDB gives most estimates as a quarter, so most bars cover three months. Orange bars are projects that HDB's own register of blocks already lists as built, in whole or in part.

Owners collect their keys once their block is finished. HDB keeps each buyer's own estimated date up to date and sends the appointment no more than two weeks ahead. Real dates can land earlier or later than the bar, and HDB finishes a project block by block. Flat counts are the numbers HDB launched.
